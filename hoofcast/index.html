How ratings work

Each day's rating looks at two things: trail footing (built from rainfall over the 3-5 days before the ride) and day-of conditions (wind, heat/humidity, cold, active rain, and storms/ice).

Footing: under 0.1" of rain in the 3 days prior is Good; 0.1-0.5" is Fair (soft); over 0.5" total, or any single day over 1" within the prior 5 days (even if that day falls outside the 3-day total above), is Poor (muddy).

Wind: under 10 mph is Good, 10-15 Fair, 15-20 Poor, over 20 mph Extreme.

Heat/Cold: heat index under 85°F is Good, 85-95°F is a caution tier, over 95°F is Extreme. Highs under 32°F are flagged cold; under 20°F is Extreme.

Frost/Ice: looks at the overnight low separately from the daytime high above, since a mild day can still frost hard at dawn. Under 33°F is Frost likely; under 33°F following rain the day before is an Icy footing risk (refreezing wet ground); under 20°F is a Hard freeze.

Rain that day: under 0.02" is Dry, up to 0.1" Slight chance, up to 0.5" Wet, over 0.5" a Washout.

Storms: any thunderstorms, freezing rain, or heavy snow in the forecast forces the day to Sketchy.

The overall rating is set by the single worst factor that day — it's always named in the breakdown below the rating.

Sunrise/sunset, daylight length, UV index, and air quality are shown for reference below the rating but don't affect it — they're informational, not safety factors for the ride itself.

Forecast and recent-rain data from Open-Meteo, a free open weather API. This is a v0.0.7 MVP — thresholds are tunable and will keep improving.
